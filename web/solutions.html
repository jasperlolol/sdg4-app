    What works

    The world is off track, but some countries have moved fast. Here is what changed, what the evidence says works, and why it is still not enough.

    The world in 2000 and in 2024

    Switch between the years, or play the change.

    Enrolment also grew: by 327 million students in primary and secondary school, by 45% in pre-primary and by 161% in education after secondary school.

    Completion and enrolment: UNESCO GEM Report 2026. Youth literacy: GEM Report 2026, Chapter 7.

    Six things that work

    Each of these has evidence behind it in the latest UNESCO reports.

    +1 year

    Make school compulsory, not just free

    In 14 African countries, making education compulsory, and not just free, added over a year of schooling.

    AccessGEM Report 2026

    36%

    Support families with cash

    Children whose families get cash transfers tied to school attendance are 36% more likely to be enrolled.

    EquityGEM Report 2026

    Half a year

    Feed students at school

    School feeding programmes have been shown to add up to half a year of learning for every US$100 spent. Meal schemes exist in 84% of countries, but many rely on donor money that is now shrinking.

    LearningGEM Report 2026

    Nearly 1 year

    Bring electricity

    In Cambodia, electrification alone has been linked to nearly a full extra year of schooling. Over 1 in 5 primary schools worldwide still lack electricity, safe drinking water or basic sanitation.

    SchoolsGEM Report 2026; UN SDG Report 2026

    1% → 24%

    Write inclusion into law

    Since 2000, the share of countries with laws that provide for inclusive education has risen from 1% to 24%. Laws saying children with disabilities should learn in inclusive settings went from 17% to 29%.

    InclusionGEM Report 2026

    76% vs 8%

    Send money where it is needed most

    76% of countries have policies to move resources to disadvantaged schools, but only 8% make the most of their funding tools to do it.

    FundingGEM Report 2026

    Countries that moved fast

    Big changes are possible, including in some of the world’s poorer countries.

    Ethiopia

    18% → 84%

    Primary enrolment rate, from 1974 to 2024.

    Nepal

    33 points

    Gap between girls and boys in lower-secondary completion, closed between 1990 and 2024.

    Cambodia

    22 behind → 11 ahead

    Girls compared with boys in primary completion, from 1981 to 2024.

    Côte d’Ivoire

    Halved

    Out-of-school rates for children, adolescents and youth, since 2000.

    China

    7% → 60%+

    Access to university and other tertiary education, from 1999 to 2024.

    Six more countries

    80% lower

    Out-of-school rates cut by at least 80% since 2000: Madagascar and Togo for children, Morocco and Viet Nam for adolescents, Georgia and Türkiye for youth.

    Sources: UNESCO GEM Report 2026; UNESCO GEM Gender Report 2026 for Nepal and Cambodia.

    2105

    Why it is still not enough

    At today’s pace, 95% of young people would finish upper secondary school only in 2105, 75 years after the deadline. Meanwhile, aid to education fell 8% between 2023 and 2024, and 113 countries spend more on debt repayments than on education.
