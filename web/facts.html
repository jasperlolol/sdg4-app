    Where the world stands

    The latest numbers on who is in school, who finishes, who learns and who gets left behind. Hover over or tap the charts for exact values.

    Access

    Sources 1, 2, 3

    273 million are out of school, and the number is rising

    One in six of the world’s school-age children, adolescents and youth were out of school in 2024. The number has gone up for seven years in a row.

    Completion

    Sources 1, 2, 3

    More young people finish school, but progress is slow

    At today’s pace, the world would reach 95% upper-secondary completion only in 2105.

    Learning

    Sources 1, 3

    Being in school is not the same as learning

    Students who reach the minimum level of proficiency by the end of primary school. This is the latest global estimate, from 2019; it has not been updated since the pandemic.

    From 59% to 47%

    The share of students with minimum reading skills fell between 2012 and 2022 in 57 mostly middle- and high-income countries with comparable data. In maths it fell from 41% to 36%.

    Equity

    Sources 1, 7

    The poorest fall further behind at every level

    Nearly 4 in 10

    countries have not reached gender parity in primary completion. At upper-secondary level it is almost 5 in 6.

    76%

    Young people in rural areas complete upper secondary school at 76% of the rate of their urban peers.

    18%

    of girls are married before age 18 (2025), down from 25% in 2000. Early marriage and pregnancy still push many girls out of school.

    Resources

    Sources 1, 3, 4, 6

    Schools, teachers and budgets are stretched thin

    Schools

    Over 1 in 5

    primary schools worldwide lack electricity, safe drinking water or basic sanitation.

    28%

    of schools in low-income countries have electricity, the same share as in 2015.

    Teachers

    1 in 5

    teachers worldwide do not meet their country’s minimum qualification standards (2024).

    15 million

    extra teachers are needed in sub-Saharan Africa alone by 2030.

    Funding

    7.5%

    of development aid went to education in 2024, its lowest share in two decades.

    113

    countries, home to 6.1 billion people, spend more on debt repayments than on education.
